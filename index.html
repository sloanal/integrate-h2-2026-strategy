A look back · H1 2026
A nod to H1 2026

This year has been
about foundations.

H1 was foundation-laying, six ways over: an AI and agents substrate, a data-graduation MVP now demonstrating IL5, CRDT-powered realtime, enterprise table stakes, a UX platform built to scale, and the groundwork to build our own integrations. Coming out of it, we have:

01
Foundation · AI & Agents

An AI foundation & first agents

The AI Data Foundation (embeddings, permission-safe retrieval, real-time sync), plus AI observability, and our first shipped capabilities: notification summaries, at-risk projects, "what's risky," and Ask Integrate v1.

02
Foundation · Data Graduation

A data-graduation MVP

integrate-data-graduation-svc is live, and we can now demonstrate graduating data into IL5. Proving that path end-to-end is the hard part: every environment above it graduates on the same machinery, so this is the foundation all future graduation builds on.

03
Foundation · Realtime / CRDT

A CRDT foundation

The MPP Merge work and CRDT service give us the substrate for real multiplayer experiences: live presence, concurrent edits, and conflict-free sync.

04
Foundation · Enterprise Table Stakes

Table stakes worthy of enterprise

Tags, custom statuses, priority, custom colors, grouping, approvals, Kanban, and baselines: the everyday essentials teams expect at scale.

05
Foundation · UX Platform

A UX foundation built to scale

Components v3 is a system that compounds our build velocity; the Gantt performance rework lets us scale to far larger, denser programs; and the new Rail navigation opens up entirely new surfaces for growth.

06
Foundation · Integrations

Ready to roll our own integrations

The Token Motel service gives us secure, managed credential and token storage for third-party connections. With that piece in place, we can build first-party integrations ourselves instead of renting them.

02 · Strategic thesis
What's changing in H2

Mostly the same,
sharper in one place.

The H1 thesis holds. Three bets keep compounding exactly as planned. What changes is who we build for first.

What stays the same
STILL THE MOAT

Data graduation leads.

Moving data across every classification boundary stays our focus and our most important moat: the differentiator no competitor can fast-follow.

PULLING AHEAD

Agentic leapfrogs us.

We host our own models instead of renting someone else's: the disciplined, peculiar choice no incumbent will make. It puts our AI on networks theirs cannot reach.

PAVING ADOPTION

Platform core UX opens doors.

The platform core and its UX keep paving the way for broader adoption, so every new surface lands faster and reaches further.

What's changing
One shift, and everything downstream of it: we move from building primarily for the Manager to building for the Leader.

The shifting persona

We spent the last year focused on the manager who lives in the schedule. As we close bigger contracts, the buyer must see direct value for themselves in our product.

05 · What this means
What does this mean

Same strategy,
re-weighted for the Leader.

To be clear: nearly everything on the roadmap stays exactly as planned. Data graduation, the agent, and the platform core all continue at full weight. Serving the Leader changes the order of just a few things.

↑ HIGHER PRIORITY

Reports

Executive reporting and brief-ready outputs move up: leaders buy visibility and outcomes, and need to report up without burning hours building briefs.

↑ HIGHER PRIORITY

Risk

Risk surfacing becomes first-class: risk flagged before it becomes slip is exactly the report-up that gets a leader promoted.

↓ TEMPORARILY DEPRIORITIZED

Budgets

EVM and commercial budgeting slide to next year: a sequencing call, not an abandonment. The Leader isn't buying budget tooling yet.
